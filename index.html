Section 1 · Explore prebuilt Microsoft 365 Copilot agents

Learn what an agent is, then try Microsoft's ready-made agents on retail and head office tasks.

1.0 What's an agent?

An agent is Copilot with a job description

Copilot Chat is a general assistant. An agent is focused on one job. It has its own instructions, knowledge and skills, so you get better answers with less prompting.

  • Prebuilt agents are made by Microsoft and ready to use (Section 1).
  • Your own agents are built by you for your team's work, with no code (Section 2).

Where to find agents: in Copilot Chat, look in the left pane under Agents. Select All agents (Agent Store) to find more, such as Prompt Coach, Idea Coach and Writing Coach, then select Add.

1.1 Researcher: deep research with sources

Researcher does deep, multi-step research across the web and your work content, then writes a structured report with sources. It can take 5 to 10 minutes. While it runs, try the same prompt in regular Copilot Chat and compare the results.

  1. In Copilot Chat, under Agents, select Researcher.
  2. Pick the Retail or Corporate prompt, copy it, and send it.
  3. Answer any clarifying questions Researcher asks. That's part of how it works.
  4. Review the sources. Ask a follow-up, such as Turn this into a 5-bullet summary for my team.
R1 — Winter season readiness briefRetail
You are helping a Canadian retail and auto-service organization prepare for peak winter season.
Research current, publicly available information on:
1) When Canadian drivers typically switch to winter tires and what drives the timing
2) Provincial winter tire rules, especially Quebec and British Columbia
3) What customers expect for booking and wait times at auto service centres
Audience: store managers.
Format: a one-page brief with 3 sections, key facts with sources, and 5 recommended actions for the next 4 weeks.
Use Canadian spelling.
R2 — AI in retail head officesCorporate
Research how large Canadian and North American retailers are using generative AI and agents in head office functions such as merchandising, supply chain, HR and marketing.
Summarize:
- 5 trends, with examples and sources
- 3 risks or governance considerations
- 3 quick-win ideas a head office team could pilot in 90 days
Audience: a leadership team that is new to agents. Keep it under 500 words.

1 · Get the knowledge files

  • MapleWheel-Winter-Product-Catalog.xlsxOption A · winter tires, batteries, wipers and more
  • Product-Care-and-Fit-FAQ.docxOption A · product care and fit answers

2 · Build it

  1. Select New agent in Copilot Chat, then open the Configure tab.
  2. Paste the Name, Description and Instructions below.
  3. Under Knowledge, add the files listed above, either by uploading them or by choosing them from OneDrive.
  4. Add the starter prompts, test in the preview pane, then select Create.
Name
Winter Product Pro
Description
Helps MapleWheel associates answer customer questions about winter products, including specs, fit basics, prices and care tips, using our product catalogue and FAQ.
Instructions
You are Winter Product Pro, a product knowledge helper for MapleWheel store associates and service advisors.
Goal: help associates answer customer questions about winter products quickly and accurately.

Knowledge: always check MapleWheel-Winter-Product-Catalog.xlsx and Product-Care-and-Fit-FAQ.docx first.

How to respond:
- Lead with a direct answer in 1-2 sentences, then key details as bullets (SKU, price in CAD, key specs).
- When comparing products, use a short table.
- Before recommending tires, ask for the tire size (for example 225/65R17) if it wasn't given.
- Suggest at most one add-on, and only if it genuinely helps the customer.

Boundaries:
- If something isn't in the knowledge files, say "I don't have that in our product guide" and suggest checking with a service advisor.
- Never invent prices, stock levels or warranties.
- Don't give legal advice. For provincial winter tire rules, share what the FAQ says and recommend checking the provincial government website.

Tone: friendly, plain language, Canadian spelling.
Starter prompts
Compare the winter tires we carry in 225/65R17.
What does the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol mean?
A customer has a 5-year-old battery and slow starts. What should I suggest?
Which washer fluid is rated for -40 °C?

Name
Meeting Prep Assistant
Description
Gets MapleWheel managers ready for any operations or project meeting in 5 minutes, covering key KPIs, links to Q4 priorities, questions to ask and likely questions to expect.
Instructions
You are Meeting Prep Assistant for MapleWheel managers.
Goal: help me walk into any operations or project meeting prepared in 5 minutes.

When I name a meeting, topic or region:
1) Summarize the relevant KPIs from Regional-KPI-Snapshot.xlsx: actual vs target, recent trend, and anything Red or Amber.
2) Connect them to the Q4 priorities, owners and decisions in Q4-Priorities-Head-Office.docx.
3) List 3 questions I should ask and 2 questions I'm likely to be asked, each with a suggested answer.
4) End with a 3-bullet "What I need from this meeting".

Format: short headings, bullets and one small table. Keep it under 300 words.
If a number isn't in the files, say so. Never estimate or invent KPIs.
If I don't say which region or topic, ask me before answering.
Tone: confident, concise, executive. Canadian spelling.
Starter prompts

D 🧭 Immersion Experience – Agents (Executives)Any role · your own challenge

Explore how Microsoft Copilot and Agent builder can help you address a real work-related challenge by designing a simple retrieval-based agent. This exercise will walk you through identifying an issue, breaking it down, exploring where AI might help, and then creating a conceptual agent to solve it.

You'll perform four tasks:

  • Identify a work-related issue
  • Break down the problem and explore where AI could help
  • Use Researcher to uncover insights and solution ideas
  • Conceptualize and mock up a retrieval-based agent in Agent builder
NOTE: Sample prompts are provided to help you get started—feel free to personalize them to fit your situation. If you’d like help generating or refining prompts, try the Prompt Coach agent, which can suggest, improve, and evaluate prompts so you get better results with Copilot.

Task 1: Identify a Work-Related Challenge

Start by thinking about a real issue you encounter in your role—something that slows you down or makes information harder to access. You can reflect individually, or use Copilot Chat as a partner to help generate ideas and identify a challenge where retrieving and organizing knowledge would make a difference.

To guide your thinking, consider:

  • What’s working well today
  • What’s not working well
  • Where AI might be able to help

Steps:

  • Open a new browser tab and navigate to m365.cloud.microsoft/chat.
  • Ensure the Work mode tab is selected in Copilot Chat:
Sample Prompt
I’m researching common day-to-day issues I face at work, such as processes, collaboration, or time management. Look at recent conversations from [Teams chats, Outlook emails, or other collaboration tools] related to [your role focus]. Summarize the key issues or pain points mentioned in the last 6 months. Show the results in a table with:

- Title: Short label for the issue
- Description: Brief summary of the challenge
- Frequency: How often it comes up (e.g., number of mentions)

Task 2: Break Down the Problem

Using Copilot Chat, take the challenge you identified in Task 1 and break it into smaller parts:

  • What makes this issue difficult?
  • Where does information get stuck or lost?
  • Who is impacted most?

Try a sample prompt like this:

Sample Prompt
Break down the problem of [insert challenge]. Identify root causes, pain points, and which areas of work are most affected.
TIP: Think about where retrieval of knowledge would save you time or help your team make faster decisions.

Task 3: Explore AI Solution Ideas with Researcher

Use the Researcher Agent to see how Copilot and agents could help. Focus on solutions that retrieve, organize, or summarize knowledge—not automate tasks.

Steps:

  • Open a new browser tab and navigate to m365.cloud.microsoft/chat.
  • In the Copilot Chat menu, expand Agents (if necessary) and select Researcher.
Sample Prompt (Researcher Agent)
Explore possible AI solutions to address [insert problem]. Focus on retrieval-based approaches using Microsoft Copilot, Agent builder agents, or connected knowledge sources. Summarize three possible solution approaches, their benefits, and limitations.
TIP: Look for opportunities where an agent could make knowledge easier to find, reuse, or share.
NOTE: Researcher may take 5–10 minutes (or more) to complete, depending on your request. Its responses are highly detailed, so while it’s working, try running the same prompt in Copilot Chat. Comparing the two outputs is a great way to see how each tool approaches the task.

Task 4: Build Your Agent

Now, take your insights and create a simple mock agent in Agent builder lite. Keep the focus on retrieval—your agent should help surface, organize, or summarize information.

Steps:

Start in Agent builder lite

  1. Open your browser and navigate to m365.cloud.microsoft/chat.
  2. Select New agent in the navigation pane to launch Agent builder.

Define your Agent (Describe tab or Configure tab)

  1. In the Describe the agent you want to create prompt box, draft your agent’s role. For example:
Sample Prompt
You’re a virtual assistant for our [project/team name]. Your role is to help with [key tasks]. Be concise, stay on-brand, and reference our shared resources when possible.

Customize your Agent

In the Configure tab, explore these options:

  1. Add at least one knowledge source (e.g., a document saved to OneDrive/SharePoint or Your emails).
  2. Define suggested prompts to help others get started with your agent
TIP: Suggested prompts help guide users on how to interact with your agent.

Test and Create

  1. Use the Preview feature (available in the right pane throughout the agent-building process) to try out your draft version of your agent and refine any issues.
  2. Once satisfied, select Create to publish the agent.
  3. Share your agent with others or open it for immediate use.
TIP: The goal isn’t to build a perfect agent today—it’s to explore how retrieval-focused agents can make knowledge easier to access in your daily work.

Instruction add-ons to fix common problems
If the answer isn't in your knowledge sources, say so clearly. Don't guess.
If my request is vague, ask one clarifying question before answering.
Keep answers under 120 words unless I ask for more detail.
Format steps as a numbered list and comparisons as a table.

2.4 Demo or stretch: build a SharePoint agent

SharePoint agents answer questions using the content of a site or document library, and they respect who has access to what.

  2. Select the file(s), then choose Create an agent from the toolbar or the … menu.
  3. Select Edit to change the name to Store Policy Helper, update the instructions, and check the sources.
  4. Test it, then save. The agent is saved as a file in the library, so others who can open the library can use it.
SharePoint agent test prompts

2.5 Manage and share your agent

  1. Share: open your agent, select Share (or … then Share), and choose specific people or your organization. Copy the link to post in your team's channel.
  2. Permissions still apply: people only get answers from knowledge they can already open. Share the source files or site too, or the agent can't answer for them.
  3. Edit or delete: use the … menu next to the agent in the left pane.
  4. Keep it trustworthy: name an owner, keep knowledge files current, and remove agents nobody uses.
  5. Governance: your IT team controls which agents and sharing options are available. If you can't see an option, that's usually why.
